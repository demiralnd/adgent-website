Running these accounts for clients? How Adgent works across a book of client accounts

Settings that teach the bidding the wrong thing.

Each one gets worse with time rather than staying flat, because each one trains the algorithm.

Call length as the conversion.

Count any call over thirty seconds as a conversion and the bidding learns to buy long calls: wrong numbers, reschedules, vendors, job applicants. It does exactly what it was told. Across more than 60 million calls, only 61% of callers to businesses spoke with a person at all.

Source: Invoca, Call Conversion Industry Benchmarks Report, June 2025.

Locations bidding against each other.

Campaigns built by service rather than by location can put several of your own offices into the same auction. You pay a premium to compete with yourself, and the account report shows nothing unusual because in aggregate it is fine. Account Architect looks for campaigns chasing the same objective inside an account; comparing one location's account with another's is roadmap.

Presence or interest, on by default.

Google's default location option reaches people in your area and people who have only shown interest in it. For a clinic with a ten-mile catchment, an out-of-area click is rarely a patient, and it is the setting nobody checks.

Source: Google Ads Help, advanced location options.

Nobody in this vertical says ROAS.

Operators say cost per new patient, cost per booked appointment, show rate, chair utilisation. Near full capacity, more budget tends to buy leads booked into distant slots, and distant slots no-show more. That number lives in your booking system, not the ad account.

What multi-location operators ask first.

We have twelve locations. Does it read all of them or just the biggest?
All of them, one at a time. Every connected account is read daily, including the ones nobody opened this week, each against its own history, so a healthy group average can't hide three sites losing money. It doesn't rank one location against another; that needs cross-account reading, which is roadmap. Decisions
Can it move budget from a weak location to a strong one?
Not across accounts. Adgent opens one company's data per session, so there is no blended judgment across the group. Within a location it prepares budget and bid moves from that account's own numbers, while the workspace shows which account needs attention first.
Google says we got store visits. Should I trust that number?
Treat it as a model, not a count. Google estimates store visits from a panel, with thresholds it doesn't publish, and since September 2025 they count as conversions by default at a value Google sets. Adgent shows which conversion actions are primary and actually driving your bidding, and a metric window the Trust Gate can't trust never raises an action. Trust Gate
What about show rate and the calls nobody answers?
Adgent can't see either: show rate and revenue per patient live in your booking or practice-management system, and there's no connection to it. What it can see is what your bidding is trained on: a short call counted as a conversion, a radius running past your catchment, placement and demographic waste.
