Media planning

A plan is only worth reading if it shows where the numbers came from.

Every media plan is a stack of forecasts, and almost none of them say what they were forecast from. A cell reading ₺1,400 CPA is either last quarter’s campaign or somebody’s optimism, and the deck does not tell you which. Adgent drafts the plan as data instead: budget split, flights, line items, and a cost range for each — with the source campaigns and the window printed underneath every range. Where the account has no history, it writes no number at all and says so.

What you are competing with

The real alternative is two days in a spreadsheet.

Nobody buys a planning tool because planning is impossible. They buy it because the plan takes a planner two days — pulling last quarter’s costs by hand, splitting budget by feel, writing flights into a grid — and by the time it is finished, nothing in it can be traced back to a campaign. The reasoning was real. It just did not survive the transfer into cells.

The plan as a deck
  • A budget split, agreed in a meeting nobody minuted
  • A CPA per line, keyed in from memory of last quarter
  • Every market carries an estimate, including the one you have never run
  • Assumptions live in the planner’s head, and leave when they do

Reviewable only by someone who already knows the account as well as the person who wrote it.

The plan as data
  • Every cost range names its source campaigns and window. You can open them and check.
  • The market with no history gets no number. It gets a two-to-four-week test instead, stated as such.
  • The measurement caveat is in the plan, not discovered in the QBR.
  • It arrives as a Draft with open questions attached — nothing is applied to any account.

Reviewable by anyone, in the ten minutes before the call.

What makes the artifact honest

Three things in the plan that a deck cannot carry.

01

Every range names its basis

A cost range is written as the campaigns it came from and the window they were read over — not as a figure with a source that lives in someone’s head. You can open those campaigns and disagree with the read, which is the only kind of forecast worth reviewing.

02

No history means no number

A market the account has never run in gets budget and a test window, and no estimate at all. The blank is the finding. Filling it with a plausible figure is how a plan starts lying in the first row and keeps the tone for the rest of the page.

03

It says what it does not know

The measurement caveat sits with the figures, not in a footnote: if the conversion action carries a value proxy rather than confirmed value, every cost below it is directional. The unresolved items stay unresolved and addressed to you by name — the opening date, the brand cap, the retargeting pool. A planner who closes those silently has made three decisions nobody will find until the quarter is running.

One account, one plan

The plan is built from the connected account’s own Meta and Google Ads history and nothing else. There is no pooled benchmark under it and no learning carried across from another advertiser — which is also why the empty line stays empty: there is no other account’s Dalmere to borrow a number from.

Where the account’s own numbers come from →

Margin and lifetime value are not in any of this by default — no ad platform reports them and no connection supplies them. If a market is worth more per booking than another, you say so in chat and the plan carries it from then on. That is the honest version: the context is yours, and it gets used, but it is not inferred from a data feed that does not have it.

Then what

A Draft is a plan nothing has happened to yet.

The plan sits as data with a status on it. Approving a line item is what moves it toward a build, and every write that follows lands in the ledger with the state it replaced — so a plan you approved in November is still a plan you can walk back in December.

Nothing is applied by drafting

Producing the plan touches no campaign, no budget and no setting. It is an artifact you read, argue with, and edit before any of it becomes a change request.

Approval is per line, not per plan

You can take the two Norvik lines and leave the Dalmere test in draft until the opening date is confirmed. A plan is not a single yes.

What ships is undoable from chat

Anything Adgent writes to an account is logged with what it replaced. To reverse it you ask in chat, it proposes the inverse change, and you approve that too.
