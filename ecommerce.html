Where the money leaks between the ad and the bank.

Three mechanics the platform report can't show you. The first one costs money every day it runs.

Ads keep spending after the shelf is empty.

A campaign selling well enough to clear your stock does not stop. It keeps buying clicks for a product nobody can buy, at the moment its history says scale it. Merchant Center connects read-only, so your catalogue, availability included, and item issues can be read for the account you pick. That read happens in chat; it isn't part of the daily analysis yet.

Returns can reorder the campaigns.

About 19.3% of online sales come back, after the month you reported has closed. Return rates are not even across campaigns: a discount-led winner can return hardest, so the ranking you budgeted against can change once refunds land. Unless you send the platform an adjustment, the original conversion stays counted.

Source: NRF, 2025 Retail Returns Landscape.

A test you can run without us.

Put the platforms' reported revenue next to your store's own figure for the same window. Do it again six weeks later. The first comparison shows how far apart they are; the second shows how much of what you reported was real, and that is the number to set budgets against.

Why does the revenue in my ad account not match what my store banked?
Because the two count different things at different times. The platform credits a conversion when the pixel fires and keeps it unless you send an adjustment; your store nets out refunds, discounts and cancellations weeks later. Share your store's revenue in a Drive file and Adgent reads the platform's figure against it, with GA4 beside both as context. A direct store connection, Shopify first, is next. Ground truth
Can it tell me which campaigns get restated by returns?
Share a refund export in Google Drive and it reads the returns next to your campaigns; where the export carries each order's source, it can say which campaigns the refunds came back from. Return rates and margins you confirm in chat are kept as account memory for later sessions.
Does it know when I am paying to advertise something that is out of stock?
Partly. Merchant Center connects read-only, so the catalogue, availability included, and item issues for the account you pick can be read in chat. That feed isn't part of the daily analysis yet, so a sold-out product that is still being advertised is not flagged on its own. Ask, and it checks.
Will it keep up during peak season?
Campaign data syncs every night, so the read is never more than a day old and a drift can be caught the next day rather than at month-end. The grain is day-level, current through yesterday; there is no hourly figure. In a season when the most expensive inventory of the year is bought in a few days, that is the difference that matters.
