What it means in practice

Tidy structure is how accounts fall under the floor.

Nothing warns you. A campaign below the threshold spends exactly like one above it: delivery stays exploratory, the cost per result swings on small numbers, and every weekly report reads as a performance story rather than a sample-size story. The platforms publish the floor and then let you run underneath it indefinitely.

The most common way in is organisation. Split one healthy campaign carrying 90 conversions a month into six tidy product campaigns and you get six units of 15 — all under Google’s evaluation floor, all thrashing, all worse than the one you started with. That mechanic, and the structure that avoids it, is in Google Ads account structure in 2026.

On Meta the same thing happens through scaling: a large budget change re-opens the learning phase, and the ad set needs the 50 events again from a standing start. How to scale a winner without breaking it covers the step size that avoids the reset.

And when the volume genuinely is not there, the useful answer is a refusal rather than a target. Adgent’s Trust Gate is that refusal in the product: it declines to build on an event the account cannot feed, using the account’s own numbers as the reason — a pixel with four purchases in thirty days is running at about one a week against a floor of fifty in seven, and no bid strategy fixes that.

  • Volume is not correctness. Clearing the floor says the optimizer has enough examples to learn from. It says nothing about whether the conversions are counted once, valued correctly, or attributed to the right click.
  • Day-one numbers are not final. Meta’s 7-day click window keeps rewriting yesterday, so a thin account looks worse in the morning than it will by Thursday. Why day-one data reads pessimistic by construction.
  • The floor is per event. Optimizing three ad sets toward three different events splits the signal three ways even when the account total looks healthy. Pick the deepest event that clears the floor, and judge the rest on the business outcome.
Frequently asked

Conversion volume, answered.

How many conversions does Meta need to exit the learning phase?
Meta's own developer documentation says to structure ad sets for a minimum of 50 events over a 7-day period, and that not meeting the minimum events threshold can increase your costs per result. The threshold is per ad set, per week, on the event you optimize for — not per campaign and not per account.
How many conversions does Google Smart Bidding need?
It depends on the strategy and the campaign type. Google states 15 conversions in the past 30 days at the conversion-tracking level as a Target ROAS requirement for Search and Shopping, 50 in the past 35 days for Demand Gen, and 30 in the past 30 days for Video Action campaigns. Separately, Google recommends evaluating performance over a period with at least 30 conversions, and 50 for Target ROAS.
What should I do if the account is below the floor?
Consolidate rather than split: three ad sets at 12 conversions a week each are all below the threshold, while one at 36 is closer to clearing it. The other two moves are optimizing toward an event higher in the funnel that does clear the floor, knowing it carries less value, and fixing whatever is dropping conversions before adding budget.
Does clearing the floor mean the numbers can be trusted?
No. Volume and correctness are different problems. An account can clear every published threshold and still report a ROAS that is wrong because the conversion value is set to gross revenue, the pixel is double-counting, or yesterday's figure has not finished maturing inside the attribution window. This check answers the volume question only.
