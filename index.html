The blind spot

The platform can't see the number that decides it.

An ad platform optimizes toward what its pixel can measure. It never sees your cost of goods, your margin, or what a customer is worth on the second order. So when it finds a campaign that converts cheaply and loses money on every order, it does the logical thing: it scales it. Nobody is asleep at the wheel — the dashboard is green, the target is met, and the report is correct on its own terms. That is the expensive part.

Reconciled against your own revenue

Adgent reads GA4, Shopify and Search Console alongside the ad platforms, so a report reflects money that actually landed — not what the pixel claimed.

A structural gap, not a feature gap

No optimizer built inside an ad platform can grade its own spend against your margin. It doesn't have the data, and it isn't neutral about the answer.

Trust Gate

The test of a thinking tool: does it ever tell you no?

Anything that only says yes is executing, not reasoning. Both refusals came from one real account, unprompted, before any budget moved — each showing the account's own numbers as the reason. A tool that checks this afterwards reports the same facts a month later, once the money has already taught the algorithm nothing.

How we're built

Touching the platform is easy. Knowing what to do is hard.

Seven layers sit between a connected ad account and a decision you'd stand behind. Most tools stop at the audit. Everything above it is the hard part.

it stacks
L6 · Operating surface

The morning brief, a queue of things worth acting on, chat, live dashboards, and a shell built for running many accounts at once.

A chat window wired to some data sources is not a product. The daily surface your team actually works in is.

L5 · Safe execution

Read-only until you say otherwise. Every write is logged with its before-state, labelled reversible or not before you approve, and undone in one click. Each company's data sits in its own database file.

Pointing a model at a live ad account is the easy part and the dangerous part. The ledger also does a second job nobody asks of it: a change you can undo and redo is a change you can settle, instead of argue about.

L4 · Judgment

Root cause rather than symptom, compared fairly across platforms, with the decision stated and the money attached to it.

The audit plus memory is what makes a position possible: not "here's what moved," but "here's what to do, and here's what it's worth."

L3 · Memory

Context that survives the session: what was looked at, what was decided, and what you've told it about how this brand runs.

Memory is what turns an audit into judgment. A generic tool restarts from zero every time; here the context accumulates, across platforms, and it's yours.

L2 · Audit

What happened and where — trends, anomalies against the account's own history and its peers, breakdowns by placement, device and geography. Then the same audit on the creative: its DNA, the story scene by scene, and where viewers drop off.

Everyone can get you to the account numbers. Reading the creative this way is rarer — but it still only tells you what happened. Nobody takes the next step for you.

L1 · Data correctness

A year of history, synced daily, with attribution normalized and conversion events reconciled into one comparable shape across platforms.

Raw platform output is not decision-ready. Skip this and you get answers delivered with total confidence and no basis — the most expensive kind of wrong.

L0 · Connectivity

Reads your accounts directly from the ad platforms, and pulls in analytics and store data alongside them.

This layer only answers "can it reach the account." It's getting cheaper for everyone — which helps us, because it isn't what we sell.

Questions

Good questions, answered plainly.

it answers
Does Adgent change my campaigns on its own?
No. Access is read-only by default. When a fix or a campaign is ready, Adgent prepares it and waits — you apply it from chat with one message. The control is always yours; nothing is automatic.
Which ad platforms does it cover?
Meta, Google and TikTok Ads, read and reasoned about as one account — and it pulls in GA4, Shopify and Search Console so reports reflect real revenue, not just platform-reported numbers.
What can I actually do from chat?
Ask about any ad, campaign, or account; run placement and demographic audits; generate custom reports; apply prepared fixes; and build cross-channel campaigns — all by message.
What is the Trust Gate?
It's how Adgent stays honest about numbers. If a campaign optimizes toward a value-less event, or a metric is still inside Meta's 7-day attribution window, we flag it rather than report a confident figure. We'd rather tell you a number can't be trusted than pretend it can.
How do I know an agent won't spam me?
You see it before you turn it on. Describe what to watch and Adgent replays it against your last 90 days — every time it would have fired, on what, and how often. Nine alerts in three months is a useful agent; four hundred is one you'd have muted by Thursday. Adjust the threshold and the answer updates, all before anything reaches you.
Will it really pay for itself?
In our audits, a single account's first pass typically surfaces meaningful recoverable spend — before counting the hours it saves your team. Talk to sales and we'll show you what Adgent finds in your account.
Who is Adgent for?
Agencies managing many accounts, ecommerce brands running their own spend, in-house growth teams, and independent performance marketers — anyone who'd rather talk to an analyst than decode a dashboard.
